# Language

*Shawaabdif az Akhshawi Uzbakhaamdif* (The speech of the Motionless Sea)

## Phonology

15 consonants, and 3 vowels that can be short or long.

### Consonants

| Labial | Alveolar | Palatal    | Velar/Uvular |
|:-------|:---------|:-----------|:-------------|
| m      | n        |            |              |
| b      | d        |            | q            |
|        |          | ʒ ~ d͡ʒ (j) |              |
| f      | s        | ʃ (sh)     | x ~ χ (kh)   |
|        | z        |            |              |
|        | r        |            |              |
| w      | l        | j (y)      |              |

### Vowels

| Short | Long     |
|:------|:---------|
| a i u | a: i: u: |

### Long vowels

A word can have either zero or a single long vowel. Some grammatical constructs (most notably the plural root) add or shift a long vowel forward in the word.

Vowels following the long vowel tend to pronounced short and close to a shwa.

### Syllable structure

- Open: CV
- Closed: CVC

The initial consonant can be dropped at the start of a word:

- Open initial: V
- Closed initial: VC

The closing consonant in a syllable (and indeed at word end) cannot be the semi-vowels *y* or *w*.

### Diphthongs and final semivowels

There are no diphthongs. When a root change would put two vowels together, a consonant appears to avoid a hiatus.

1. when exactly one of the two vowels is an *i*, the linking consonant is *y*
2. else before an *u* comes *kh*
3. otherwise it is *w*

|       | *-a*  | *-u*   | *-i*  |
|:------|:------|:-------|:------|
| *a-*  | *awa* | *akhu* | *ayi* |
| *u-*  | *uwa* | *ukhu* | *uyi* |
| *i-*  | *iya* | *iyu*  | *iwi* |

Since *w* and *y* cannot appear at the end of a syllable or word, if a root change would do so, they reduce to:

- *w* → *f*
- *y* → *q*

Though not a strict phonological constraint, *y* rarely follows a consonant. Most accents pronounce *iy* instead. In written forms, the difference is a single diacritic:

- *dalya* vs. *daliya*

### Plural root change

Nouns, adjectives and verbs have a singular root (the basic dictionary form) and a plural root, derived via sound inversion and vowel lengthening in the final syllable.

- Open: C V → VV C
- Closed: Ca V Cb → VV Ca Cb i

This can cause the apparition of the linking consonant and reduction of a final semivowel.

- *adifi* (son), *adiwiif* (sons)
- *badaa* (sand), *bawaad* (sands)
- *iyaazdif* (service), *iyaziidfi* (services)

## Grammar

### Nouns/adjectives

They exist in singular and plural form. Plural form is simply the plural root.

A noun appearing without article is indefinite. The definite article *a* (the) comes before the noun it qualifies. *a* is often omitted with possessive pronouns or when there is no ambiguity.

They take an object marker when they are the direct or indirect object in the sentence, which harmonizes with the vowel in the last syllable:

- After a vowel V: *-rV*
- After a consonant: VC: *-Vr*

- *a mazdi siwaa akhshara*: The camel drinks dirty water.
- *a alzuwir fura maziidir iwiisha*: The desert heat burns our camels.
- *ishi marufi misif mazdiri isha*: I play music for my camel.

Adjectives normally come after the noun they qualify and agree with the noun they qualify in number and object marker.

- *a udar fashmi bakhma*: The Green River flows.
- *ishi azaara a udarar fashmiri*: I worship the Green River.

### Personal pronouns

The plural forms are regular and use the plural roots of the singular ones. There is no gender distinction, but the third person has two forms: animate (people and things that move by themselves), and inanimate (dead people and static things).

The object forms are used as usual for direct and indirect objects. Possessives function as either adjectives (placed after the noun they qualify) or nouns. Possessives agree in number and animatedness with the possessor, and are otherwise invariable.

Note: the object forms are irregular.

|                       | Subject  | Object     | Possessive |
|:----------------------|:---------|:-----------|:-----------|
| 1st sing.             | *ishi*   | *ishir*    | *isha*     |
| 2nd sing.             | *bij*    | *bijir*    | *bisha*    |
| 3rd sing. (animate)   | *zif*    | *zifir*    | *zifaj*    |
| 3rd sing. (inanimate) | *aba*    | *abar*     | *abaj*     |
| 1st plu.              | *iwiish* | *iwiishir* | *iwiisha*  |
| 2nd plu.              | *iibji*  | *iibjir*   | *iibsha*   |
| 3rd plu. (animate)    | *iizfi*  | *iizfir*   | *iizfij*   |
| 3rd plu. (inanimate)  | *awaab*  | *awaabar*  | *awaabaj*  |

Possessives, and even object pronouns are often omitted in spoken language when context is sufficient.

### Prepositions

- *az*: of (possession, provenance, material)
- *mi*: in, inside of
- *uzmi*: outside of
- *ib*: to, towards, into (location) / into (transformative case)
- ??: at (location near or around, not enclosed)
- *wid*: time indicator (at the moment of)
- *dar*: from (location, person) / by, because of (agent of passive clause, source or cause, tool or method, etc.)
- *na* + object marker: to (generic indirect object marker, recipient)
- *misif* + object marker: for (beneficiary of the action, purpose, intention)
- *ashli*: all, all of (countable or uncountable)
- *fid*: with (accompaniment, manner), about, regarding (topic)

All these prepositions are followed by nouns or nominal clauses. To introduce a subordinate clause with an action, use *daz* (see below).

### Conjunctions

- *wa*: and
- *uzwa*: but, yet, however
- *dana*: like
- *daz*: that (starts a subordinate clause)

### Verbs

They agree in number with their subject, that is: they take the plural root if the subject is plural.

The first verb group (most common) is *-ad*, the second is *-iC*, and the third is irregular verbs.

#### Tenses

There are three main tenses: present (actions going on at the time of speech, or timeless), perfect (actions completed or in the past), and unreal (future actions, uncertainty, conditional, wishes, etc.)

In the singular, verbs lose their final consonant to form the present. In plural, the plural root is used instead. Perfect is then formed by adding *-wa*, and the unreal tense by adding *-li*.

| Verb group   | Present     | Perfect             | Unreal                |
|:-------------|:------------|:--------------------|:----------------------|
| *-ad*, sing. | *-a*        | *-awa*              | *-ali*                |
| *-ad*, plu.  | plural root | plural root + *-wa* | plural root + *-li*   |
| *-iC*, sing. | *-i*        | *-iwa*              | *-ili*                |
| *-iC*, plu.  | plural root | plural root + *-wa* | plural root + *-li*   |

Verbs are always in the active voice, except when using the past participle.

#### "To be"

Two irregular (third-group) verbs have the meaning of to be, depending on whether the state is temporary or permanent:

- *fiya*: current state, location, or temporary condition. Singular *fi*, plural *izif* (perfect *fiwa*/*izifwa*, unreal *fili*/*izifli*).
- *shuda*: identity or inherent characteristic. Singular *shu*, plural *ushuq* (perfect *shuwa*/*ushuqwa*, unreal *shuli*/*ushuqli*).

Both are irregular: singular and plural root do not derive from the infinitive, though *-wa* (perfect) and *-li* (unreal) attach as usual.

- *a mazdi fi mi a badaawi*: The camel is in the desert.
- *a alzura shu zula*: The sun is primordial.

Both are often omitted in spoken language or informal settings, when there is no ambiguity.

- *ishi mi a azaarbi*: I'm in the temple.
- *azaar ali*: God is great.

#### Participles

The active present participle is: plural root + *-f*, regardless of number. It can act as an adjective, the pivot in a relative clause or a noun (the act related to that verb). It can be used to emphasize an action going on.

- *a mazdi siyaafdif*: the drinking camel
- *a mazdi fi siyaafdif*: The camel is drinking.
- *adifi iyaazdif ilwiri zifaj iza a badaawiri*: A son serving his family serves the desert.
- *mawaazdif usha ayiizmiri*: Travelling breaks people.

The passive past participle is: singular root without final consonant + *-n* for singular, and plural root + *-n* for plural subjects. It marks the state of having been the object of the action. It normally acts as adjective, more rarely as noun. It can introduce a passive clause (usually followed by *dar*: by).

- *mazdifi ufsuban*: a cursed road
- *a iyaazdin*: the served (ie. the masters)

Only transitive verbs admit a past (passive) participle. The equivalent construction for intransitive verbs is done using a relative clause, or to use the active present participle if the meaning is similar.

- *a alzura bashi ujiwa uz fura*: The hidden sun (lit. "which hid") does not burn.
- *a alzura uyiijnif uz fura*: The hiding sun does not burn.

#### Imperative

For the second person, an injunction told to the person talked to, the verb in its basic form (infinitive) is used by itself.

- *marufiq birsharufur na iwiishir*: Play us a lullaby.
- *shuda zalij misif ilifara*: Be good to your sister.

The unreal tense can be used for any person, by implying a subordinate clause for "I wish", or "Would that", with or without the conjunction *daz*:

- *irshuwi irili*: Let rain fall (lit. "rain will/would fall")
- *daz iwiish ushuqli ayiizli*: Let us be free (lit. "that we would be free")  

### Adverbs

Adverbs are not a separate word class. To qualify an action, use *fid* and a noun (with) or add an adjective to the subject of the action when context is clear.
 
- *a mazdi siwaawa ashli siwaara fid dakhba*: The camel quickly drank all the water (lit: with speed).
- *a mazdi dakhbaj siwaawa ashli siwaara*: The quick camel drank all the water.

### Sentence construction

#### Word order

The usual word order is SVO (subject verb object), with SOV employed more informally or for emphasis.

#### Negative clauses

Negative sentences are formed by placing *uz* before the word to negate, which is often the verb, but not only (for emphasis or precision). The prefix *uz-* comes into the construction of many antonyms that don't have a simple form.

#### Interrogative clauses

Open questions are formed by placing the word *biwib* wherever the answer would functionally fit in the question. *biwib* takes object marks and plural roots regularly.

- *biwib shaba*: Who speaks?
- *bij shabawa biwibir*: What did you say?
- *mazdifi az biwib uji*: Whose caravan is hiding?
- *wid biwib*: when, at what time, during what?
- *na biwibir*: to whom?
- *mi biwib*: where, in what?
- *misif biwibir*: for whom, for what purpose?
- *dar biwib*: from where, from who, why, because of what?
- etc.

Yes-no questions don't have a grammatical equivalent, they are always rephrased as open questions. Spoken language sometimes uses the declarative sentence with intonation to imply a question.

#### Relative clauses

Similarly, relative clauses can be introduced with the relative pronoun *bashi*, and is always at the start of the relative clause, possibly with a conjunction:

- *a ayiizmi bayiish mawaazdi uz azawaardi sarimadar*: The men who travel do not worship Serimet.
- *wid bashi*: when ("at the time which", "during witch")
- *na bashiri*: to whom
- *mi bashi*: in which
- etc.

#### Object subordinate clauses

They are introduced by verbs like *sad* (to wish), *shabad* (to say), or *ladad* (to think), for which the object of the action is an entire clause. They start with the conjunction *daz* (that). Those that represent uncertain actions are in the unreal tense.

- *zif shabawa daz bij ufsubawa zifir*: She said that you cursed her!
- *ishi sali daz zif suwili*: I wish he loved me. (lit: I would want that he loves)

Note: *daz* is never omitted.

#### Other subordinate clauses

They are built using the usual prepositions and introducing the clause with *daz*.

Epression of time of another action: *wid daz* (when, at the time that)

- *zif ladawa fid bij wid daz zif yasawa marufur*: She thought about you when she heard the music. (lit.: "during that she...")

Expression of purpose, intention: *misif daz* (in order to, so that)

- *ishi mazawa ib alishandariya misif daz ishi namali a alwasibbiri*: I travelled to Alexandria to find the university (lit: so that I would find)

Expression of cause: *dar daz* (because):

- *sayiirmi sariyaamdi dar daz udar bakhma*: The sprouts grow because the river flows. (lit: because of the fact that the river flows)

### Misc. expressions

#### Quantifiers

The quantifiers *uza* (none), *iifaq* (some), *adaawa* (a lot, many) and *ashli* (all, whole) apply to both countables and uncountables.

Logically:

- singular countable refer to divisions of a single object: none of it, some of it, a lot of it, the whole thing
- plural countable refers to a collection of objects: no objects, a few objects, many objects, all the objects
- uncountable (normally in the singular) refers to an amount: no amount, a little amount, a large amount, all of it 

Using uncountables in the plural is usually a way to make them countable, referring to different kinds or variations.

Note that *ashli* is also an adjective (entire, whole) and a noun (entirety) and in these forms inflects regularly.

## Lexicon

Some common word constructions:

- *-ad*: noun to verb, the action related to that thing
- *-wi*: noun to noun, collective, collection, sometimes place of many
- *-bi*: noun to noun, location of
- *-j(V)*: noun to adjective, having the quality of
- *-zim*: person
- *uz-*: negative, opposite, anti
- *al-*: large, great
- *az-*: in family names, indicate provenance
- *-m*: device, construction, object

## Phrases

lit: All human beings were born free and equal with worth and right. Mind and conscience were granted to them. They would act towards others with a mentality of brotherhood.
